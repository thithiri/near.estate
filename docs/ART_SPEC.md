# Art specification

The street has a 300 logical-pixel reference height. Plots have a 170px pitch; facades are centered in a 140px-wide envelope with bottom anchor (70,160), placed at street y=190. Sidewalk y=190–206, parking lane y=206–246, driving lane y=246–290.

Each facade source is a transparent 560×640 PNG (4× logical 140×160). Visible pixels must fit height limits 70/100/130/160px at levels 1–4. All projections must stay inside the 140px width. `packages/shared` validates metadata envelopes; decoded pixel verification is still required in the media pipeline.

Types and paired silhouettes: Retail asymmetric store/sawtooth hall; Cafe glasshouse/timber pergola; Restaurant curved-roof diner with round street windows/courtyard bistro; Office glass block/cantilevered studio; Homes stepped terrace/narrow townhouse. Four palette accents: coral, sky blue, mint, yellow. Six car bodies in four colours fit 64×32 logical pixels. Logo, billboard and text render separately from facade art.

Reference sheets: [distinct player buildings](images/near-estate-player-buildings-distinct-v2.png) and [car bodies](images/near-estate-cars-v1.png). These are concept sheets, not validated transparent sprite assets.

The first playable preset library is generated by `scripts/generate-presets.mjs`. It produces 40 transparent 560×640 facade PNGs, 40 matching white palette masks, 40 140×160 thumbnails, and a hash catalog in `apps/web/src/preset-catalog.json`. The [preset contact sheet](images/near-estate-preset-contact-sheet-v1.png) shows the ten level-2 silhouettes. `npm run assets:check` decodes every facade and mask, checks alpha bounds against the four level envelopes, verifies thumbnail dimensions, placement rectangles, hashes, and the complete type/variant/level grid. The client adds owner name, logo initials, and approved ad content separately from the base image.

The [all-level neighbour review sheet](images/near-estate-level-neighbours-v1.png) places each of the 40 base sprites between the same game-owned burger stop and level-1 home at the 170px plot pitch and y=190 baseline. Run `npm run assets:review` to rebuild it after changing preset art. This sheet checks silhouettes and spacing; the street renderer applies the palette masks and live text.

The curated fallback now gives each type a visible material cue within those envelopes: striped shop awnings and sawtooth market bays; glazed cafe ribs and timber pergola lamps; diner marquee lights and bistro details; stronger framed office glass; and residential rails, gables and planting. Keep these cues behind the separate logo, billboard and name layers. Preset sprite and mask URLs include a short content-hash query so a new catalog does not reuse a browser-cached older image at the same filename.

The [phone street capture](images/near-estate-curated-street-phone-v5.png) at 667×375 and [desktop street capture](images/near-estate-curated-street-desktop-v5.png) at 1280×720 show the regenerated presets in the actual guest renderer, including signs, curb spaces, cars and the game HUD. The local art pass increases type contrast and gives levels 3–4 additional architectural features; it does not change the 40-shape grid or palette-mask placements. The burger billboard is a guest-only sample. These are browser viewport checks, not physical-device measurements.

`scripts/generate-world-sprites.mjs` creates the six car bodies in Red, Blue, Yellow and Green as transparent 256×128 PNGs (64×32 logical pixels), plus five transparent 560×640 game-owned destination sprites. Each car uses a center anchor at (32,16) so driving and parked examples share the same alignment. The [car and civic asset sheet](images/near-estate-world-sprites-v1.png) shows all six shapes and five roles. The same asset check verifies their decoded bounds, hashes and complete body/colour/role set. Civic labels render separately from the art.

Phone gameplay uses landscape. Menus and studio allow portrait. See [V1_SPEC](V1_SPEC.md#screen-layout-and-orientation) for viewport and safe-area acceptance.
