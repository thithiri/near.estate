## What reads well now

- The five building types already have different rooflines and footprints, and the two variants within each type use different structures.
- Level changes are visible in the neighbour sheet without crossing the height limits or covering curb spaces.
- The logo circle, rule sign, parking stalls and main action are consistently placed in the street scene.

## What needs improvement

1. **Material identity at gameplay scale.** The first local pass gives the five types different wall or glass colors and adds foliage to the greenhouse cafe. At 667 × 375, the roofline differences survive, but more type-specific details are still needed. Use brick and display glass for retail, timber or greenhouse ribs for cafes, enamel and marquee lighting for restaurants, framed glazing or concrete bands for offices, and warm plaster with balconies or planting for homes. Keep the four player palette choices as accents, so a palette change does not erase the type.
2. **Variant separation across levels.** Both illustrated level-2 shapes now change massing, roofline and entrance placement; Retail and Cafe keep that separation through all four levels. Carry the same differences through new level-1, level-3 and level-4 art for Restaurant, Office and Homes, then compare both variants at the same level in a real street row before accepting them. Preserve the existing 140px width, level height caps and approved logo/billboard rectangles.
3. **Upgrade character.** Levels 3–4 now add a type-specific accent, including shop gable windows, cafe vines, a diner awning, office roof detail and home balconies or planters. The restaurant change reads in the latest phone and desktop captures. Review the remaining accents on a physical phone and decide whether level 2 also needs a distinct feature. Keep level 4 recognizable as the same player's building.
4. **Approved advertising in context.** The guest street screenshots show placeholder initials and empty ad space. Capture a current game street with an approved logo and billboard, its owner name and its curb rule, then review whether the ad remains legible beside intersections and other buildings. The operator must approve the exact media revision before this capture is used as publication evidence.
5. **Street depth and focus.** The phone scene has a large uniform sky and a light background row. Add restrained foreground details and varied background silhouettes only after the building surfaces read clearly; the player's car, two curb stalls and rule sign must remain easy to find.
